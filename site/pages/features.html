Features

What the knight acts out, and how it looks.

What it shows

It's made to be glanced at from across the room, not read like a dashboard. It is not a monitoring tool: no alerts and no history. Keep your monitoring; this is the part you glance at. A problem takes over the scene, with a headline saying why. Small events play out while all is quiet. The colour of the words is the status: green when quiet, amber for a warning, red when critical, blue while something is at work.

  • An advert is blocked

    AdGuard Home, Pi-hole or Technitium

    A fly buzzes in and the knight swats it. Indoors, the cat catches it.

  • fail2ban bans someone

    fail2ban

    A goblin climbs over the castle wall, gets bonked on its saucepan and vanishes in a puff of smoke.

  • A download is imported

    Sonarr, Radarr or Lidarr

    A crate floats down on a parachute, with the title in the caption.

  • The Raspberry Pi goes quiet

    A second machine

    The raspberry pie is gone, down to its last slice, and the knight stares at the empty dish.

  • A machine is under strain

    This machine, or any device through Prometheus, Beszel or Glances

    The knight trains flat out in the yard, red in the face and sweating. The headline names the machine and the resource, such as NAS MEMORY 93%.

  • A container is unhealthy

    Docker

    The knight takes to bed with a thermometer, and the headline names the container.

  • A disk is filling up

    This machine

    Down in the cellar, the knight struggles with a heavy crate.

  • Someone opens an SSH session

    This machine

    The knight is called to court to talk with the monarch.

  • Updates are running

    A job you name, such as nightly maintenance

    The knight hammers at the anvil in the smithy.

  • A device goes down

    A NAS, a camera, a VM, anything with a port

    Each device stands in the scene as a medieval thing: an owl, a crystal ball, a potion. When one goes down it breaks, and a question mark blinks over it.

  • Something is streaming

    Plex, Jellyfin, Tautulli or Tracearr

    A television appears in whatever scene is showing, and the knight stops by now and then to watch it. The line under the headline says who is watching what.

  • The internet drops

    An address you choose

    The bell rope on the battlement is cut, and the knight is puzzled.

There's more: an attack, a backup that's late or has just finished, and night, when the knight goes to bed and the screen dims. With nothing to report, the knight patrols the battlement, the camp, the house, the training yard and the smithy in turn.

The knight and the world

The look is chosen on the display itself. Press and hold the screen for two seconds and the settings open.

  • Three builds: Classic, Broad and Slim
  • Five weapons, from a greatsword to a crossbow that fires
  • Six skin tones, six hair colours and five hair styles
  • Six tabards and six plumes
  • Shields, helmets and armour, and a cloak or a cape
  • Three monarchs: a king, a queen and a monarch with no title
  • A calico cat or a ginger tabby

You also choose which quiet scenes take turns, and where some problems play out: running hot by the camp fire instead of in the yard, say.

A world sets the scenes, the cast, the words and their colours in one go. The medieval world is the one included. The pack format is documented for the curious; a normal page loads only packs the project has signed.

  • Broad, mace and round shield
  • Slim, crossbow and kettle hat
